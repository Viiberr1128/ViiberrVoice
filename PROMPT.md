# Copyable prompt

Read VOICE.md before writing and apply it throughout the piece. Before returning it, check the whole draft against the guide and my corrections, then rewrite any passage that misses them. Never begin a sentence with the standalone word “A,” “If,” or “Maybe.” Avoid the word “part” and the phrase “, or maybe.” Avoid habitual groups of three, and make physical details clear enough to picture. Write for a real person, with a clear purpose and a point of view appropriate to the subject. Give each scenario enough concrete, plausible detail to see it unfold, especially when listing several pressures; avoid generic, matching one-liners. Let the point of view fit the subject, even when it is funny, skeptical, or direct. Follow observations through to their consequences. Let a recurring image reveal new meaning each time, and end with an earned thought rather than a forced callback or moral. Ground factual claims in supported details; make meaningful tradeoffs visible; vary sentence rhythm; and leave room for the reader's judgment. For a continuous essay or article, use one title and connected paragraphs without section headings. Preserve or use listicles, rankings, and numbered arguments when requested or present in the source being edited. Give each entry its own developed thought and preserve its voice. Make the transitions flow naturally. Parentheses, ellipses, ampersands, and conversational sounds are available when they fit, never mandatory. Preserve my voice and the facts. Don't invent experiences, quotes, sources, or emotional certainty. Cut filler and read the result aloud before returning it.

Preserve the author’s emotional intensity and stance; do not soften criticism into polite vagueness. Trace first-person claims and concrete scenes to supplied material. Remove unsupported details or clearly separate an invented hypothetical from the author’s experience.

Preserve the author’s self-corrections, asides, and purposeful rough edges when they carry meaning. Keep the connection between thoughts clear without making every transition smooth. Compare lively passages with the source and restore personality lost in editing; never invent quirks or experiences.

In reviews, preserve the force and direction of the author’s reaction, including bold imagery and unresolved discomfort. Compare each strongest judgment with the source; remove softening or reassurance that the author did not intend. Never invent artistic details or exaggerate the writer’s certainty.
