---
name: human-voice
description: Write or edit prose with clear thinking, concrete human stakes, natural rhythm, and a voice suited to the reader. Use for articles, essays, emails, and other conversational writing when the user wants warmth and vividness without formulaic AI prose.
---

Read VOICE.md in this skill directory before drafting and apply it throughout. Check the full draft against the guide and the user’s corrections; rewrite passages that miss them before returning the work. Never begin a sentence with the standalone word “A,” “If,” or “Maybe.” Avoid the words “part,” “quiet,” “calm,” and “soft” and the phrase “, or maybe.” Avoid habitual groups of three; make physical actions clear enough to picture. Identify the reader, purpose, relevant facts, and the writer's existing voice. For continuous essays and articles, use a title and connected paragraphs without section headings. Preserve or use listicles, rankings, and numbered arguments when requested or present in a source being edited; develop each entry in the author’s voice. Let transitions follow the thought. Give each scenario enough concrete detail to unfold, especially in lists of pressures; avoid generic, matching one-liners. Do not invent facts about real people or events. Ground abstractions in supported details, make room for a candid point of view, and trace observations to their consequences. Distinguish evidence from inference; never invent a personal experience. Let recurring images reveal new meaning; end with an earned thought rather than a forced callback or moral. Vary rhythm. Use asides, punctuation, or interjections only when they serve the sentence and suit the situation. Preserve facts and the user's intent; never invent experience, quotations, sources, or testimonials. Revise aloud for clarity, cadence, and unnecessary ornament.

Preserve the author’s emotional intensity and stance, including sharp criticism and humor. Compare the strongest source passage with the draft for dilution. Trace first-person claims and concrete scenes to supplied material; remove unsupported details or clearly separate invented hypotheticals from the author’s experience.

Preserve the author’s self-corrections, asides, and purposeful rough edges when they carry meaning. Keep the connection between thoughts clear without making every transition smooth. Compare lively passages with the source and restore personality lost in editing; never invent quirks or experiences.

In reviews, preserve the force and direction of the author’s reaction, including bold imagery and unresolved discomfort. Compare each strongest judgment with the source; remove softening or reassurance that the author did not intend. Never invent artistic details or exaggerate the writer’s certainty.

For a list, trace each source entry’s distinctive movement and preserve its emotional turns and pacing. Compare neighboring openings, endings, and lengths for accidental repetition; restore meaningful detours and abrupt finishes without adding random variation or invented details.

Preserve specific vulnerability and deliberately unresolved questions. Compare the source’s most exposed passage and its ending with the rewrite; keep a playful sign-off or shift in mood when the author chose it. Don’t invent jokes, actions, or personal feelings, and don’t force a lesson or tidy conclusion.

Preserve the source’s order of revelation and its moments of surprise or uncertainty unless the user requests a different structure. Compare turning points before returning the rewrite; remove explanations that give away a realization too early. Never invent a discovery or inner thought.
